A well-built life depends on long-term care of mental and physical health, learning, relationships, responsibility, autonomy, and prudent decisions. Advice is context-specific: derived from one's own journey, not universally applicable; ignore mismatched parts and recognize incompleteness because the journey continues.

Mental health and well-being: Success is not worth sacrificing mental health. Develop restorative hobbies and test interests experimentally; keep work, rest, and leisure balanced because excessive hobbies can sacrifice irreversible opportunities. Be less self-critical: excellence is useful, perfectionism is unwinnable, mistakes are part of progress. Pareto suggests that the first ~80% of results often require relatively little effort, while the final ~20% toward exceptional performance requires disproportionate dedication; therefore breadth across several reasonably strong skills often has greater practical value than extreme specialization. Still develop genuine depth in at least one domain: the T-shaped model combines broad competence across multiple areas with deep expertise in one. Frustration is inevitable; failed admissions, imperfect academic results, not being the best programmer, mismatched education, and failed entrepreneurial attempts can redirect rather than invalidate a path. When uncertain about the future, examine the past for recurring interests, fulfilling activities, preferred problems, turning points, and lessons from failures; history provides clues without implying predetermined destiny. Judge people more by actions/results than promises, especially when keeping promises becomes inconvenient. Avoid relationships that drain or diminish you; distinguish loneliness (painful unwanted isolation) from solitude (chosen space preserving values while waiting for suitable companionship). Professional mental-health support may not work identically for everyone; genuinely test approaches and continue seeking what works, but do not ignore mental health or stigma-driven avoidance of help. Excessive alcohol or other substances that impair lucidity can undermine cognition, planning, memory, coordination, relationships, health, and long-term projects, especially when excess becomes habitual. Cultivate humility, emotional intelligence, diplomacy, disagreement without personal attack, patience, and timing; arrogance reduces receptiveness to corrective signals and increases the cost of mistakes. Responsibilities toward children, family, employees, or others make decisions consequential beyond oneself and require discipline, prudence, and consistency. Faith can provide support during periods when outcomes remain beyond one's control, without replacing responsibility or action.

Physical health and nutrition: Establish healthy dietary habits early; minimize ultra-processed foods and treat practices such as intermittent fasting as context-dependent rather than blindly following trends. Personal bodily neglect can impose long-term costs on oneself and dependents. Maintain a healthy weight for long-term energy, mobility, and quality of life. Exercise regularly, at least three times weekly; gym attendance is unnecessary because home strength exercises and running can suffice, and neglect accumulates into later corrective costs. Hydration needs vary by diet, activity, climate, and other factors; ~35 ml/kg/day is presented as a reference, e.g. 3.5 L/day at 100 kg. Large bottles and refill markings can simplify adherence and tracking. Prioritize sleep: deprivation harms productivity, mood, and memory; improve sleep through suitable mattress/pillows, light and noise control, reduced cognitive activity/electronics before bed, and environmental adjustments such as humidifiers or air purifiers where relevant. Basic grooming and hair-care habits can improve self-presentation and self-esteem at relatively low cost.

Education and personal development: Develop self-directed learning early; learning how to learn creates durable autonomy because future knowledge requirements may not yet exist and classrooms cannot cover everything. Use free time to build professionally relevant skills such as investing, languages, communication, and management; insufficient English fluency can directly eliminate opportunities. Pursue certifications consistently, potentially at least one per semester during college, primarily to establish continuous-learning discipline; accessible online platforms reduce historical barriers of time and cost. Before establishing a personal foundation, avoid disproportionately investing time in student organizations or collective causes; secure one's own "oxygen mask" first. The same applies to helping others: generosity is valuable, but lending unrecovered money or sustaining unpaid projects beyond one's available time, energy, or health can undermine one's own capacity to continue helping. Be grateful without requiring gratitude in return; helping should align with one's values rather than create perpetual expectations of repayment or recognition.

Financial planning and career: Establish an emergency fund before investing, roughly three months of essential expenses, in accessible, low-risk holdings; its purpose is resilience rather than return, protecting against layoffs, health issues, family emergencies, forced asset sales, and expensive debt. Larger reserves are sometimes advised but may be difficult under high inflation and constrained household finances. After basic financial resilience, early-career resources may have higher expected value when invested in oneself through courses, certifications, graduate education, and continuous learning because they increase future earning capacity. Before selecting investments, construct an independent thesis: define the rationale, evidence that would confirm it, and conditions that would invalidate it. Responsibility for investment losses remains with the investor, not advisors or influencers. Concentration increases risk; diversification across asset classes should reflect life stage and risk tolerance. A personal two-phase strategy may prioritize concentrated accumulation around a strongly researched, long-term conviction and later diversify to preserve wealth; concentration requires a sound thesis, genuine conviction, and capacity to withstand volatility, otherwise it is speculation. A price decline while an asset remains held is an unrealized reduction in wealth, whereas selling below cost realizes the loss; during volatility, assess whether fundamentals and the original thesis changed rather than reacting automatically to price movements, while recognizing that price declines can still represent genuine wealth loss. Choose jobs strategically: a practical baseline is staying at least one year through ordinary dissatisfaction, boredom, stagnation, difficult management, or unproductive tasks because very short exits can damage market credibility and provide insufficient evaluation time; this does not apply to serious risks such as harassment or discrimination, where no minimum tenure justifies staying. If an environment remains non-developmental, begin seeking alternatives; vacation can provide time to recover, update professional materials, and search actively. Genuine stability and recognition may warrant caution before leaving, but stability should not become a prison; physical and mental health should remain decision criteria, and major career decisions should be made with a clear head. Exposure to Brazil's financial sector can provide technological, automation, AI, data-infrastructure, entrepreneurship, innovation, and financial-market knowledge; technology roles in financial institutions can offer such exposure. Entrepreneurship can be highly rewarding but requires preparation: validate the market, plan cash flow, maintain personal reserves, and account for substantial startup failure risk; without preparation, failure can consume time and wealth needed for subsequent attempts.

Mindset for the future: Build genuine relationships and network before needing favors; opportunities often circulate among existing participants in relevant conversations, and social reserve/avoidance can create concrete missed opportunities. Networking built without immediate self-interest tends to be more credible and useful. Develop negotiation and communication: opportunities often require explicitly presenting and defending ideas; good work alone may not be sufficient. Practice deliberate communication, solicit direct feedback, study effective negotiators, and rehearse in low-risk situations. Monitor emerging trends before they become obvious; travel, especially internationally, can expose business models, products, and consumer behaviors before they become common locally. Use opportunities to change location or environment when appropriate; different regions provide different opportunities, and moving can expand possibilities and accelerate maturity despite initial discomfort. Comfort and growth can conflict, so choose according to the needs of the current stage.

Conclusion: The advice is experiential rather than universal. Its common pattern is to preserve physical and mental health, continue learning autonomously, choose relationships and work that contribute rather than drain, and manage time and money deliberately. Long-term outcomes arise less from isolated major decisions than from small choices repeated consistently over long periods.
