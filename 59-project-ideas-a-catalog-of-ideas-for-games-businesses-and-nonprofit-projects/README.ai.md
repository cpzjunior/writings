Project-Ideas is a public catalog of concepts for games, businesses, and nonprofit initiatives developed over several years. Initially focused on game development, the collection emerged from experimentation with tools such as MonoGame and Godot. Although some concepts reached the prototyping stage, limited time and the multidisciplinary expertise required for game production prevented their completion. The collection subsequently expanded to include business opportunities and social initiatives, with proposals ranging from preliminary ideas to more structured concepts involving mechanics, systems, and narratives.

The catalog is intended as a source of inspiration and collaboration rather than a portfolio of validated or implementation-ready projects. Its publication on GitHub reflects the principle that an idea should not be constrained by its creator's ability to execute it. Other individuals may have the skills, resources, or perspectives needed to develop these concepts into projects or incorporate them into existing initiatives. Each proposal should be independently assessed in terms of its target audience, value proposition, feasibility, execution requirements, and potential impact.

The author is particularly interested in collaborating with developers and designers, contributing primarily through conceptualization, project structuring, and solution architecture, while other participants provide expertise in game design, art, narrative, and development. Collaboration may result in a commercial product, a prototype, or simply the exploration and refinement of a concept. All proposals are published in the Project-Ideas GitHub repository under the Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International (CC BY 4.0) license, permitting sharing, adaptation, and commercial use with appropriate attribution, without requiring permission or compensation.

The twenty selected game concepts illustrate different approaches to mechanics, narrative, simulation, management, and fictional world-building. They are presented according to the author's personal preferences rather than objective assessments of quality or commercial potential.

Just Breathe: Restarting One Step at a Time is a narrative adventure about a young hikikomori attempting to overcome social isolation and regain independence. Progression is based on rebuilding autonomy and self-esteem through everyday activities, beginning with personal hygiene, organizing living spaces, and establishing routines before advancing toward greater responsibilities and social interactions. Unlike conventional progression systems, character development is nonlinear, allowing setbacks and insecurities to reverse previous achievements. The central mechanic transforms personal recovery into gradual, interactive progression.

Project Hope: The Seed of Change is a social management simulation in which a lottery winner establishes an organization to improve the lives of orphans and homeless people. The initiative gradually expands into housing, education, healthcare, and vocational training. Players manage financial resources, infrastructure, and individual needs while dealing with bureaucracy, prejudice, conflicts, and unexpected crises. Each beneficiary has distinct abilities, experiences, and aspirations, requiring personalized approaches rather than standardized assistance. Success is measured not only by organizational growth but also by helping individuals regain independence and eventually support others.

Vessel Conflict: The Masquerade of Centuries is a science-fiction thriller centered on two brothers capable of transferring their consciousness into other human bodies, assuming their identities and abilities. The older brother uses this power to infiltrate institutions and establish global influence, while the younger initially attempts to stop him. Their conflict spans centuries and gradually challenges the distinction between their opposing moral positions. Consciousness transfer enables espionage, infiltration, manipulation, and historical exploration, while the narrative examines identity, morality, and the consequences of adopting an adversary's methods.

Play The Symphony: An Orchestral Experience is a musical game in which players become members of an orchestra rather than individual performers. They select instruments from different musical families and must follow sheet music, maintain tempo, control dynamics, and synchronize with the conductor and other musicians. A career mode includes rehearsals, auditions, performances, and competitions. Evaluation emphasizes collective execution rather than individual accuracy, making coordination essential. Online cooperation allows multiple players to perform different instrumental roles within the same orchestra.

Family Matters: Wealth & Governance is a contemporary business dynasty simulation inspired by the political intrigue and succession mechanics of Crusader Kings. Players assume leadership of a wealthy family, managing companies, investments, properties, and relationships across generations. Family members have individual ambitions, alliances, and conflicts that influence corporate decisions and succession. Players must delegate responsibilities, prepare heirs, negotiate with influential stakeholders, and resolve internal disputes. Long-term success depends on preserving both the family's wealth and its organizational structure through generational transitions.

The Debt Collector: Dead Assets Pay No Debts is an intergalactic action and management game in which players work for a bank recovering loans across planets, space stations, and colonies. Debtors include individuals, criminals, corporations, and organized crime groups. Players can negotiate, intimidate, fight, and employ other coercive methods, but must never kill a debtor, since their outstanding debt would otherwise become the collector's responsibility. Each operation requires evaluating risks and choosing appropriate collection strategies. Career progression introduces increasingly valuable contracts and reveals connections to political interests, criminal organizations, and interplanetary conspiracies. The concept combines space exploration, combat, negotiation, and financial humor.

Mindjack: Borrowed Lives is a psychological thriller and survival game in which players operate for a clandestine service that remotely controls the minds of people facing dangerous situations. Through encrypted communications, they guide ordinary individuals through kidnappings, pursuits, escapes, and other emergencies. Each person has distinct physical and psychological limitations. Although the service prohibits questioning clients' actions or circumstances, operations gradually reveal inconsistencies and morally ambiguous situations. Players must plan, improvise, and explore environments to ensure survival while confronting the possibility that saving someone may not necessarily constitute the right decision.

Drone Saga: Undercover Ace combines drone piloting, competitive sports, and espionage. The protagonist begins as an amateur enthusiast participating in local competitions before attracting the attention of police and military organizations. His piloting abilities lead to clandestine reconnaissance, infiltration, and intelligence missions, creating a double life between professional competitions and secret operations. Players improve piloting skills, customize equipment, and manage their public reputation while balancing tournament performance with operational secrecy. The gameplay alternates between speed and precision challenges and missions requiring discretion and planning.

Around the World in 8 Days: The Influencer Challenge is a strategy and time-management adventure in which an influencer attempts to travel around the world within eight days to establish herself as the greatest content creator. Players visit different countries, publish real-time content, complete local challenges, and compete for followers and engagement. Success requires balancing travel logistics, content production, creativity, and public image. Unexpected events, rival interference, and viral opportunities can influence progress, while algorithmic behavior and reputation crises introduce additional uncertainty. Social media dynamics become the foundation of a competitive resource-management system.

Hamster vs. Capybara: Whisker Warfare is a humorous military strategy game featuring two factions of small animals fighting for territorial dominance. Hamsters specialize in agility, improvisation, and creative equipment, while capybaras emphasize resilience, organization, and collective defense. Battles occur in miniature environments inspired by everyday human spaces, where ordinary objects become military equipment. Players manage resources, construct bases, recruit units, develop equipment, and organize offensive and defensive operations. Faction asymmetry encourages different strategies, while environmental interactions and unpredictable events create chaotic battlefield situations.

Rocket & Olive: Fast and Furries is a cooperative platform and action game starring Rocket, a raccoon, and Olive, an otter, who must stop a mad scientist threatening the world. The characters possess complementary abilities that influence exploration, combat, and puzzle-solving. Players switch between them to overcome obstacles, use gadgets, access otherwise unreachable areas, and defeat enemies. The concept combines traditional platforming with cooperative mechanics, themed environments, creative inventions, and boss encounters, drawing inspiration from Donkey Kong Country, Ratchet & Clank, and It Takes Two.

Star Piracy: Return of the Scoundrel is a space exploration RPG with management elements. Its protagonist is a Golden Age pirate who becomes frozen during an Antarctic expedition and awakens centuries later in a technologically advanced civilization. Finding the new world excessively automated and orderly, he decides to become an intergalactic pirate. Gameplay includes planetary exploration, space combat, trading, smuggling, ship customization, crew recruitment, and treasure hunting. Relationships with factions and decisions during expeditions influence reputation and opportunities. The concept transfers the freedom, discovery, and unpredictability of traditional pirate adventures into a science-fiction setting.

Shabby Casino: The House Always Loses is a business management simulation in which an eccentric gambling enthusiast inherits a nearly bankrupt casino in Las Vegas. Players must renovate facilities, hire employees, attract customers, manage gaming operations, and compete against established businesses. The central source of humor is the owner's compulsive gambling, which creates conflicts between personal interests and the casino's financial stability. Professional gamblers, inspectors, eccentric employees, and unexpected events introduce additional management challenges. The concept combines economic simulation with satire about entrepreneurship and financial irresponsibility.

Wizard's Debt: Student Loan Hell is a fantasy RPG and management simulation about a magic student trapped by an abusive loan from a goblin bank. His soul serves as collateral, and he must repay the debt before accumulated interest triggers its seizure. Players generate income through unconventional jobs, including cleaning sewers with magic, delivering pizzas through teleportation, and serving supernatural customers. Dungeon exploration, combat, and treasure hunting offer more profitable but dangerous alternatives. Equipment, potions, repairs, and other expenses further complicate financial management, while interest continues accumulating. The concept satirizes student debt, predatory contracts, and employment difficulties through a fantasy setting.

Transient Realms: The Impersistent World is a fantasy RPG featuring a procedurally generated universe in which reality constantly changes. Cities alter their layouts, locations disappear, people assume different identities, and historical events are replaced. The protagonist alone remembers fragments of previous versions of reality, eventually discovering that his family and personal history have vanished. Procedural generation affects not only geography but also characters, cultures, relationships, and entire communities. Players investigate the phenomenon while navigating a world where achievements and personal connections may disappear without warning. The central narrative explores impermanence, memory, identity, and the difficulty of finding meaning in an unstable reality.

Ghost Office: Post-Mortem Management is a supernatural business simulation about a company whose founder dies without preparing successors or establishing an effective management structure. Unable to accept the organization's collapse, his ghost remains in the office and continues running the business by possessing employees and occasionally customers. Possession allows players to compensate for individual limitations, increase productivity, influence negotiations, and resolve operational problems. Management activities include departments, projects, meetings, financial resources, and corporate crises. The founder's inability to relinquish control remains a central source of humor, highlighting the organizational problems caused by centralized decision-making, poor succession planning, and excessive leadership dependency.

Anhangabaú: The Devil's River is an action-adventure game combining dark fantasy and supernatural horror beneath São Paulo's Anhangabaú Valley. An ancient supernatural river, sealed underground for generations, contains a black substance capable of materializing nightmares, transforming creatures, and distorting reality. When its seal breaks, the corruption spreads through tunnels, underground passages, and foundations. Players assume the role of a guardian from a forgotten lineage tasked with preventing the entity from reaching the surface. Gameplay combines interconnected underground exploration, supernatural combat, mystery investigation, and character progression. Ancient entities known as the Colossi of the Devil's River provide large-scale boss encounters, while environmental corruption progressively transforms familiar locations into hostile spaces. The concept draws inspiration from Dark Souls, Bloodborne, and Shadow of the Colossus, applying their design influences to a distinctly Brazilian urban setting.

The Anti-Mage: Justice in the Shadows is a dark fantasy action RPG with investigative and narrative elements, set in Universum Arcanum. It follows Alden, a young man from a humble background who lives in a society dominated politically and economically by mages. After awakening to magic and making a pact with a mysterious creature, he gains anti-magic abilities that allow him to neutralize magical protections, disrupt magical traces, and investigate otherwise inaccessible locations. He operates both within institutional structures, investigating crimes and corruption, and outside them, using his powers for clandestine operations against individuals beyond conventional justice. The narrative follows his rise to archmage while exploring political conflicts, investigations, faction disputes, and the long-term consequences of his decisions. His growing power gradually transforms his moral perspective, leading him to believe that he alone should determine when established rules can be ignored. A partially developed narrative framework provides additional detail about Alden and the political conflicts of Veridion.

The Saga of Eldrin: The Cursed Crown is a medieval fantasy narrative RPG set in Velmoria, an empire fragmented into seven rival kingdoms after the death of its last emperor. Eldrin, an orphan who begins as a street thief, is introduced to commerce and diplomacy by a wealthy merchant. Through ambition and intelligence, he accumulates wealth and property, gradually ascending through the aristocracy and eventually becoming a military commander and duke. His growing influence threatens the traditional nobility, leading to betrayal, the loss of his titles and possessions, and the discovery that he may be the last descendant of the imperial bloodline. His subsequent attempt to reclaim power involves conspiracies, wars, and personal losses. Rather than concluding with his acquisition of the throne, the narrative follows his transformation into a paranoid and ruthless ruler who ultimately perpetuates the conflicts that destroyed the empire. The gameplay combines RPG progression, property management, trade, diplomacy, dynastic intrigue, and military strategy, drawing inspiration from Crusader Kings, Mount & Blade, and The Witcher. The complete narrative has already been developed.

The Lord of the Glitches: Bug of the Year Edition is an adventure, action, and comedy game built around video game bugs and glitches as intentional gameplay mechanics. Inspired by unexpected behaviors such as flying mammoths in Skyrim, the concept follows an ordinary gamer transported into a video game multiverse, where he encounters MissingNo, the legendary glitch creature from Pokémon Red & Blue. Together, they investigate anomalies threatening different gaming universes. Each world parodies a recognizable game franchise and incorporates characteristic bugs, exploits, and technical inconsistencies. Players must discover and exploit these glitches to manipulate physics, bypass obstacles, and solve challenges. Examples include flying creatures and broken physics in a Skyrim-inspired world, invisible walls and abnormal ship behavior in an Assassin's Creed-inspired setting, and clipping, bomb hovering, and wrong warps in a reimagining of The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time. The narrative also satirizes excessive standardization and monetization through a corporation attempting to control the multiverse. The concept is particularly significant to its creator because it was the first game idea formally documented and remains the most personally meaningful, originating from the fascination with unexpected behaviors that make video games memorable.
